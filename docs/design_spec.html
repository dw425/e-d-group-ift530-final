E & D Group — Database Design Spec

Online game live-ops analytics

IFT 530: Advanced Database Management Systems · Final Project · Ethan Oxley, Daniel Warren · Arizona State University

Overview

Our project builds the analytics database behind an online free-to-play game — the kind of system a live-ops team actually runs on. We instrument every player action: sessions, levels played, items used, ads watched, purchases, and the location the player was in when they did it. Players arrive through marketing campaigns, so acquisition cost rides along on the user record and we can answer whether a campaign ever paid for itself. Accounts sit on a plan — free, Plus, Premium — which lets us compare free versus paid behavior rather than just free versus paid revenue.

We landed on a star schema: six dimensions around three fact tables, plus one audit table. Geography is the piece we are most pleased with — a role-playing dimension referenced five times, including both a user's registered location and their last-seen location. That single choice gave us fraud detection almost for free.

DatabaseE_D_Group on SQL Server 2025 Developer Edition
ModelStar schema — 6 dimensions, 3 facts, 1 audit table (10 total, the assignment cap)
Integrity15 foreign keys, 10 primary keys, 3 unique constraints, 32 check constraints, 7 defaults
Window30 days — 2026-09-26 through 2026-10-25, spanning two calendar months
Volume10 rows per dimension, 20–50 per fact table (as the assignment requires); schema designed for 100,000 users

DimProduct10 ProductID PK, ProductName, ProductCategory, ListPriceUSD, IsActive, ReleaseDate Store catalog, kept apart so a price change does not rewrite history
DimCampaign10 CampaignID PK, CampaignName, Channel, CostModel, StartDate, EndDate, BudgetUSD Marketing attribution and channel spend

Fact tables

TableGrainRowsCarries
FactUserEventOne row per player action48 SessionID (degenerate dimension), EventType, EventTS, LevelNumber, Outcome, DeviceType, SourceSystem

DimPlanAudit is the tenth table, written only by the trigger. It has no foreign key back to DimPlan on purpose: a DELETE audit row has to outlive the row it describes, and a foreign key would either block the delete or cascade the history away.

Relationships

Create order (drop order is its exact reverse):

  DimGeography, DimPlan, DimProduct, DimCampaign   (no outbound FKs)
    -> DimUser
         -> DimAccount
              -> FactUserEvent, FactPayment, FactSubscription

  DimPlanAudit                       (standalone, written by the trigger)

Nothing closes a loop, so the script is re-runnable. Dimension keys are assigned explicitly so the populate section can reference them deterministically; fact keys are IDENTITY, because at the volume this schema is designed for nothing should be assigning keys by hand.

Design rules we held to

  • Every coded column is constrained. 32 check constraints — no free-text status, channel, device or event-type columns anywhere.
  • Indexes are for the target volume, not the sample. Six non-key indexes on the two timestamp columns and the hot join columns. At 10 users the optimizer ignores them and scans every time; they exist because the schema is designed for 100,000.
  • Descriptive data lives in dimensions. The fact tables carry integer keys, one timestamp and short coded columns; everything readable is reached by a join.
  • Prices are snapshotted, not referenced. FactPayment.AmountUSD is what was actually charged, so a later catalog price change cannot rewrite revenue history.
  • Re-runnable. Drops child-first, rebuilds parent-first; verified over three consecutive runs, 13 PASS / 0 FAIL each time.
Making ten rows say something. Ten players over a short window will not demonstrate a reporting database by accident, so the data is laid out deliberately rather than drawn at random. The window crosses a month boundary so the monthly procedure returns two rows. Twelve sessions sit on nine days, three of them carrying more than one player, so daily active users moves between one and three instead of being flat. Nine accounts are open across both a free and a paid plan. Level five is lost three times in four. Three payments land in countries the account holder was nowhere near minutes earlier. Each of those is something a view, the procedure or the cursor depends on, and the generator asserts every one before writing a single INSERT.
